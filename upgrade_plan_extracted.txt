Pillar I: Certifiable Avionics and Run-Time Assurance for Civil and Defense Aviation

The foremost barrier preventing artificial intelligence from revolutionizing commercial aeroplanes and defense avionics is the fundamental incompatibility between data-driven machine learning and traditional aerospace design assurance. Under RTCA DO-178C / EUROCAE ED-12C (Software Considerations in Airborne Systems and Equipment Certification) and RTCA DO-254 / EUROCAE ED-80 (hardware design assurance), certification authorities demand deterministic proof that airborne software implements its high-level and low-level requirements and nothing else—a principle summarized by avionics regulators as "no surprises". Conversely, deep neural networks and reinforcement learning policies are synthesized empirically from training data rather than explicit functional requirements, leaving individual layers, neurons, and weight matrices inscrutable to human line-by-line review and incapable of satisfying conventional structural coverage verification. To make the AASI repository viable for endorsement by the Union Minister of Civil Aviation and deployment across Indian commercial carriers and indigenous aircraft programs, the codebase must be refactored around a dual-assurance paradigm combining design-time process compliance via EUROCAE WG-114 / SAE G-34 (ED-324 / ARP6983) and onboard execution bounding via ASTM F3269-21 Run-Time Assurance (RTA).

Constitutional Onboard Governance and ASTM F3269-21 Architecture

Rather than attempting the intractable task of certifying a multi-million-parameter neural network directly to DO-178C Design Assurance Level A (DAL-A), the upgraded AASI architecture must implement the ASTM F3269-21 standard (Standard Practice for Methods to Safely Bound Behavior of Aircraft Systems Containing Complex Functions Using Run-Time Assurance). Under ASTM F3269-21, the overall aircraft or spacecraft is designated as the "Larger System," while the deep learning or reinforcement meta-learning module is isolated as the unassured "Complex Function". This architectural decoupling permits the utilization of commercial-off-the-shelf (COTS) hardware and rapid design iterations of the complex neural function both during and after initial airworthiness approval without invalidating the underlying certification basis.

To operationalize this framework in production code, AASI must adopt and expand the five-stage, process-isolated cognitive loop pioneered in advanced spacecraft safety prototypes (propose \rightarrow authorise \rightarrow execute \rightarrow observe \rightarrow record), splitting each stage across strict operating system process boundaries. At the apex of this hierarchy sits the Sealed Constitutional Layer, where an immutable governance specification and an Ed25519-signed sealed boot image reside entirely outside the neural network's addressable memory space. While the AASI reasoning engine can propose flight trajectories, thruster burns, or airspace rerouting across more than 55 specialized subsystem tools spanning orbital mechanics, attitude control, collision screening, and life support, only the deterministic constitution can authorize execution. The repository must hard-code 19 explicit failsafes (F-1 through F-19) that continuously screen for prompt injection, command token forgery, GNSS or sensor spoofing, resource ceiling violations, two-person authorization rules, and operator console compromise.

Operating alongside the constitutional kernel in accordance with ASTM F3269-21 Variants 1 and 2, an independent Safety Monitor process—written in a memory-safe systems language and utilizing a completely different algorithmic family than the primary AI—continuously evaluates both the Complex Function output and the Larger System state vector. This monitor executes a 2-of-3 voting protocol on every actuator-impacting command, retaining absolute authority to veto the AI proposal, command the RTA Switch, and transition the craft into a deterministic Recovery Function (or graceful capability degradation mode) certified to traditional DO-178C standards without ever executing commands itself. Crucially, in strict compliance with ASTM F3269 Table 1 guidelines, unassured data generated by the Complex Function is never routed directly into the Safety Monitor's state estimation without independent sensor corroboration, ensuring that compromising the AI leaves the constitution, the monitor, and the tamper-evident audit log intact.

For all supervised and offline-trained machine learning constituents within AASI, the development pipeline must simultaneously align with EUROCAE WG-114 / SAE G-34's ED-324 / ARP6983 process standard, which reached a mature draft phase in Q1 2026. The repository must structure its verification artifacts into three formal tiers: the System Level governed by SAE ARP4754A / ED-79, the AI/ML Constituent Level governed by ED-324, and the Item Level governed by DO-178C and DO-254 alongside the DO-331 model-based design supplement. For vision-based landing (VBL) and perception modules, AASI must formally define both a System-Level Operational Design Domain (ODD) and an Image-Level Machine Learning Constituent ODD (MLCODD) parameterized by exact geometric and domain-specific variables, backed by automated verification of Data Quality Requirements (DQR) covering source suitability, completeness, representativeness, and accuracy.

Pillar II: Physics-Informed Neural Operators and Real-Time Aeroelasticity

A comprehensive aerospace AI platform must not only fly aircraft safely; it must fundamentally accelerate how subsonic, transonic, and hypersonic vehicles are designed and analyzed. A critical historical lesson from the defunct AASI Jetcruzer program illustrates why high-speed physics surrogates are indispensable. Although the Jetcruzer 450 achieved historic FAA Part 23 type certification on June 14, 1994 as the world's first spin-resistant aircraft—surviving over 350 spin-entry flight tests and meeting stringent 26 g crash-survivable seating requirements—the commercial Jetcruzer 500 program collapsed after consuming $70 million and 20 years of development without producing a marketable aircraft. The aircraft suffered from unresolvable coupled design flaws across its graphite-composite fuselage and swept canard configuration, including severe thermal management difficulties cooling its aft-mounted Pratt & Whitney Canada PT6A-66A pusher engine (flat-rated to 634 kW / 850 shp), excessive cabin and exterior noise, a dangerously high stall speed, and intractable center-of-gravity shifts. Traditional sequential wind-tunnel testing and rigid-body Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD) could not iterate rapidly enough across multidisciplinary aerodynamic, thermal, and structural trade spaces to rescue the program. Today, conventional Reynolds-Averaged Navier-Stokes (RANS) and Large Eddy Simulation (LES) solvers—such as OpenFOAM using the SIMPLE algorithm or second-order implicit finite volume Euler solvers—still require between 60 seconds and 1 CPU-hour per single airfoil simulation on a 221 \times 51 C-grid mesh. Furthermore, standard CFD simulations assume a rigid wing, creating severe discrepancies against real-world flight because they neglect the real-time aeroelastic coupling between structural flexibility and unsteady aerodynamic loads.

Because standard Fast Fourier Transforms (FFT) require a uniform Cartesian grid, conventional FNO architectures struggle with complex 3D aircraft fuselages, wing-body junctions, and unstructured point clouds. Consequently, AASI must implement Geo-FNO, a geometry-aware discretization-convergent framework that uses an end-to-end learned deformation neural network to deform arbitrary irregular input domains and non-uniform meshes (such as shock-adapted 221 \times 51 C-grids) into a uniform latent mesh within a unit cube where FFT executes natively. A known vulnerability of pure spectral neural operators is accuracy degradation across sharp spatial features—specifically the rapid velocity gradient inside the thin boundary layer near the airfoil surface, the leading-edge suction peak, the separated wake at high angles of attack near stall, and transonic shocks on out-of-distribution supercritical airfoils such as the NASA SC(2)-0714. Because spectral truncation discards high-frequency modes to evolve only dominant large-scale motions, fine-scale near-wall eddies are lost. By upgrading the solver core to an Implicit U-Net Enhanced Fourier Neural Operator (IUFNO), AASI decomposes the flow field into large-scale energy-containing structures learned by an implicit looping Fourier layer—which substantially reduces trainable parameter count and GPU memory consumption—and small-scale wall-bounded turbulent fluctuations captured via symmetrical U-Net encoder-decoder skip connections. In Large Eddy Simulations (LES) of 3D turbulent channel flows, IUFNO consistently outperforms standard FNO in both training convergence and long-term velocity statistics prediction.

2. Autonomous Rendezvous and Multi-Docking: From SpaDeX to Chandrayaan-4 and BAS-01

A critical operational vulnerability for ISRO—highlighted in national aerospace assessments in October 2026—is that docking test delays and proximity drift anomalies pose a direct schedule and cost risk to Chandrayaan-4 and the Bharatiya Antariksh Station. During ISRO's historic SpaDeX (Space Docking Experiment) mission—which launched on December 30, 2024 aboard PSLV-C60 with two 220 kg IMS-1 class satellites, SDX01 (Chaser) and SDX02 (Target), into a 460–470 km circular orbit before re-entering Earth's atmosphere over the South Pacific and North Atlantic Oceans on October 12–13, 2026—the spacecraft experienced greater-than-expected drift during a maneuver to reach 225 meters separation following a non-visibility period. This post-eclipse drift anomaly forced ISRO to postpone the planned January 9, 2025 docking attempt before achieving initial docking on January 16, 2025, undocking on March 13, 2025, and completing a second autonomous docking and inter-satellite power transfer demonstration in April 2025.

Mastering zero-drift, fully autonomous docking without ground-in-the-loop intervention is now an existential requirement across three flagship ISRO programs. First, SPADEX-2, which has received programmatic approval from the Government of India and awaits financial sanction, will utilize larger satellite buses and wider docking ring geometries to replicate autonomous rendezvous and docking inside a highly elliptical orbit, directly mimicking the orbital dynamics required for lunar sample return. Second, Chandrayaan-4 (targeted for launch around 2028) aims to soft-land near permanently shadowed regions of the lunar south pole—requiring surface slopes below 10^\circ, boulders smaller than 0.32\text{ m}, 11 to 12 days of continuous sunlight, and a 24 \times 24\text{ m} safe landing grid within a 1 \times 1\text{ km} zone—to collect and return up to 3 kg of lunar regolith. Because the integrated five-module spacecraft stack—comprising the Propulsion Module (PM), Lander/Descender Module (LM), Ascender Module (AM), Transfer Module (TM), and Re-entry Module (RM)—weighs between 9,200 kg and 9,600 kg, it exceeds the 8,000 kg payload limit of a single LVM3 launch vehicle. Consequently, ISRO must execute a dual-launch strategy within a one-month window, deploying two separate stacks that must autonomously rendezvous and dock in an elliptical Earth orbit prior to trans-lunar injection, followed by a second autonomous docking in Low Lunar Orbit (LLO) after the Ascender Module lifts off from the lunar surface to transfer the vacuum-sealed sample container to the Transfer and Re-entry modules. Third, the Bharatiya Antariksh Station (BAS-01) (approved by the Union Cabinet in September 2024 for launch by 2028) and Gaganyaan scale the indigenous two-motor Bharatiya Docking System (BDS) from SpaDeX's 450 mm diameter port up to an 800 mm diameter port, demanding high-precision composite spacecraft attitude control and automated power transfer.

1. Spaceborne Edge Compute and Radiation-Tolerant AI Execution

A foundational hardware bottleneck in aerospace systems is that traditional radiation-hardened (rad-hard) processors rely on redundant circuits and insulating substrates that require years to develop and flight-qualify, rendering them computationally obsolete before launch—exemplified by the fact that a commercial Apple Watch possesses 16 times the memory of the central processor aboard NASA's Mars 2020 rover. Conversely, flying commercial-off-the-shelf (COTS) silicon in orbit exposes high-density neural network weights to Total Ionizing Dose (TID) degradation and Single Event Upsets (SEUs) caused by cosmic rays and trapped protons.

The upgraded AASI systems architecture resolves this dilemma through a unified hardware-software co-design built on indigenous Indian flight heritage. On the hardware front, AASI directly leverages the MOI-TD technology demonstrator validated on ISRO's PSLV-C60 POEM-4 mission, which successfully characterized an indigenously developed nanosatellite On-Board Computer (OBC) equipped with a dedicated AI accelerator, reaction wheels, magnetorquers, and spectral sensors, while demonstrating live AI-model uplink from ground stations and on-orbit execution. For larger orbital platforms such as BAS-01, AASI can deploy inside radiation-shielded, thermally managed COTS server enclosures modeled on Hewlett Packard Enterprise's Spaceborne Computer-2 and OrbitsEdge LEO micro-data center architectures, which utilize umbrella-like solar arrays and radiative heat rejection to run unmodified production workloads in orbit. By ingesting real-time orbital radiation dosimetry from payloads like SpaDeX SDX02's Radiation Monitor (RadMon)—which builds a sovereign database of TID and SEU events for human spaceflight—AASI can dynamically trigger weight-checksum scrubbing and Triple Modular Redundancy (TMR) process voting whenever a spacecraft traverses high-radiation zones.

On the software systems front, running long-context multimodal reasoning models on edge avionics or sovereign ground stations without exhausting memory requires replacing standard transformer inference with the four system-level optimizations developed under the ASISys (System Research for Artificial Super Intelligence) framework. Specifically, AASI should integrate Adrenaline to disaggregate attention computation from feed-forward layers and boost GPU resource utilization and throughput; AdaSkip to perform adaptive sublayer skipping that accelerates long-context LLM inference during time-critical flight emergencies; SparseServe and TileSparse to unlock hardware parallelism for dynamic sparse attention across massive telemetry streams; and DualMap to enforce simultaneous cache affinity and load balancing across distributed ground-station clusters. Critical Research Gaps and Unsolved Frontier Challenges

To position AASI not merely as an engineering integration project, but as a premier scientific breakthrough worthy of joint ISRO, DRDO, and Ministry of Civil Aviation research grants, the technical plan must explicitly solve four frontier research gaps identified across the 2025–2026 aerospace and artificial intelligence literature.

1. Scalable Formal Verification and Completeness Metrics Under DO-178C and ED-324

As documented by the FAA, NASA, and Collins Aerospace within SAE Committee G-34 and EUROCAE WG-114, traditional requirements-based verification cannot evaluate empirical neural network weights, existing formal verification tools fail to scale to large inference models, and the global aviation industry lacks a mathematical metric to demonstrate "completeness of testing" or the absence of unintended behavior in high-dimensional image-based input domains. Developing a Certifiable Neuro-Symbolic Verification Wrapper within AASI that bounds neural Lipschitz stability constants and maps Image-Level ODD (MLCODD) geometry parameters directly to DO-178C Tables A-3 through A-7 verification objectives will resolve a primary bottleneck blocking Level 1–3 airborne machine learning approvals.

2. Spectral Boundary-Layer Truncation and Shock Generalization in Neural Operators

While standard FNOs and Geo-FNOs achieve exceptional speedups on smooth pressure distributions, spectral mode truncation causes accuracy degradation across sharp spatial gradients—most notably the thin velocity boundary layer adjacent to the airfoil surface, the leading-edge suction peak, separated wakes near stall, and transonic shock waves on out-of-distribution supercritical airfoils (such as the NASA SC(2)-0714) where high-angle-of-attack wind-tunnel data are absent. Formulating an Active-Learning Physics-Informed IUFNO that combines U-Net multi-scale skip connections with Theodorsen aeroelastic modal constraints—and automatically triggers localized OpenFOAM LES/RANS mesh generation whenever PDE residual uncertainty spikes across a shock boundary—addresses a fundamental open challenge in computational fluid dynamics.

3. Autonomous Relative Navigation Across Post-Eclipse Non-Visibility Regimes in Elliptical Orbits

The drift anomaly encountered at 225 meters separation during ISRO's SpaDeX mission following an orbital non-visibility period underscores the vulnerability of conventional relative navigation filters when ground tracking and solar illumination are interrupted. Extending AASI's 6-DOF Reinforcement Meta-Learning RODP filter to maintain sub-centimeter state custody across the highly elliptical orbits required for SPADEX-2 and Chandrayaan-4—relying exclusively on Inter-Satellite Link (ISL) phase measurements below 5\text{ km} and lead-free MEMS inertial rate sensors (validated on POEM-4's LEXS payload)—directly solves ISRO's highest-priority orbital docking risk.

4. SEU-Resilient On-Orbit Foundation Models on Unhardened COTS Silicon

Bridging the performance gap between obsolete radiation-hardened processors and modern AI accelerators in space requires algorithmic fault tolerance against Single Event Upsets (SEUs). Designing Fault-Aware Sparse Attention Kernels (extending ASISys/SparseServe and AdaSkip) that detect and mask bit-flipped transformer attention heads in real time—correlated against live orbital dosimetry from SDX02's RadMon payload—will enable ISRO to execute large multimodal reasoning and visual-servoing models reliably on low-cost COTS accelerators aboard the Bharatiya Antariksh Station.
